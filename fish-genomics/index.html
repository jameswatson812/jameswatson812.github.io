Fish genomics

Population genomics of the tidewater goby (Eucyclogobius newberryi), a small estuarine fish endemic to California coastal lagoons and listed under the U.S. Endangered Species Act. Its populations are subdivided among fragmented lagoon habitats, which makes it a natural system for asking how differentiation is distributed along the genome.

Genome-wide FST in 1 kb windows

Weir & Cockerham's θ among the five northern coastal units (North Coast, Greater Bay Area, Central Coast, Conception, LA/Ventura; 233 fish) in non-overlapping 1 kb windows across the 22 chromosome-scale scaffolds. Windows need at least 3 SNPs to be plotted. The dashed line marks the 99.9th percentile of window FST (0.980); 235 windows lie above it, 140 of them overlapping 132 genes.

How to read these

  • FST here is a differentiation measure among the five northern units, not a diversity measure: a window near 1 is one where the units carry different alleles. The South Coast unit (E. kefauveri) is not included, so nothing on this page describes North versus South.
  • At roughly two high-quality SNPs per kb, 1 kb windows are at the edge of what the data support. Read the median window FST (0.077) rather than the mean (0.203), and treat the outlier set as the product rather than the window-by-window trace.
  • Of 464,252 windows with at least one SNP, 234,379 have three or more and are plotted.

Data

  • Outlier windows (TSV): the 235 windows above the 99.9th percentile, with overlapping genes.
  • All windows (gzipped TSV, 7.6 MB): scaffold, start, end, SNP count, FST, outlier flag and genes for every window with at least one SNP.

Method: per-unit allele counts at high-quality biallelic SNPs, combined per window as the ratio of the summed among-unit variance component to the summed total (Weir & Cockerham 1984). Figures rendered with plotly for R.
